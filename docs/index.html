Privacy

Your logins never leave your computer.

Extrovert has no server and no analytics. It only talks to the sites you add, to Google to sign you in, and to GitHub to check for updates.

Google shares only your name, email and photo

Used to create your workspace. Extrovert never sees your password and keeps no Google tokens.

Logins belong to their workspace

Sign out of a workspace, and every login and bit of data in it is deleted from your computer.

Sign in with your browser, safely

On macOS, copy the logins you already have in Arc, Chrome and others. It runs only when you click Import, and macOS asks you first.

Install

Up and running in two minutes.

Download from GitHub Releases. Extrovert isn't signed with a paid Apple or Microsoft certificate yet, so your system asks once.

macOS

Open the .dmg and drag Extrovert to Applications. If macOS says it can't check the app, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

Windows

Run the Setup.exe. If you see "Windows protected your PC", click More info → Run anyway.

Linux

Make the .AppImage runnable with chmod +x, then open it. It updates itself.

Then sign in with Google, pick your apps, and log in to each one, or bring over the logins from your browser. A short tour shows you around.

FAQ

Questions.

Is it really free?

Yes. Every feature, unlimited accounts, unlimited websites, any number of computers. Extrovert is open source under the MIT license, with no paid plan.

Does Extrovert change the sites or read my messages?

No. It shows each site exactly as it is and never changes or automates its pages. It only uses what any browser knows: page titles (for unread counts), the sites' own notifications, and which page you're on.

Why do I sign in with Google?

Each Google account becomes its own workspace, with its own logins. Google shares only your name, email and photo. Your Google apps (Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Meet, Chat) then open as that account.

Can I use two accounts of the same app?

Yes. Add WhatsApp twice, or three Gmails. They're grouped together in the sidebar and tagged so you can tell them apart, and each keeps its own login.

Is "Sign in with your browser" safe?

It reads the logins of only the services you pick, from the browser and profile you pick, and only after macOS asks for your permission. Nothing is sent anywhere. If a copied login expires, Extrovert copies it again from the same browser; logging out of the service stops that. It works on macOS with Arc, Chrome, Brave, Edge, Vivaldi, Opera and Chromium.

Will I miss notifications?

Not if you keep Extrovert running: every service stays live in the background, with badges and native notifications. If a service signs you out, Extrovert tells you right away instead of quietly showing zero.

How much memory does it use?

Each service is a full web page, like a browser tab. Extrovert loads them one at a time, puts idle feeds to sleep when memory runs low, and lets you pause whole workspaces. The task manager shows what each service uses.

How do updates work without a server?

New versions are published on GitHub Releases. Extrovert checks there at startup and every 6 hours and tells you when one is ready. The check sends no personal data.
